# Ravenswood

Blood on the Clocktower as a cosy 3D village. Everyone is an animal walking around town, talking with
proximity voice chat: people close by hear you, people across the square don't, and buildings are
private. The Storyteller floats around as a ghost, runs the grimoire and visits players' homes at night.

It's a static site on GitHub Pages, published from the `docs/` folder. There's no server and no
logins: the Storyteller's browser hosts the game, and everyone connects straight to each other.

## Playing

1. **The Storyteller opens the site** and chooses **Host a game**. The invite link is under the room
   code at the top left.
2. **Players open the link**, type their name and pick an animal and colour (43 to choose from, from
   wolf and fox to axolotl, snail, squid, a bat that flaps its wings, jellyfish, gecko, ferret, dugong,
   sea cucumber, ibis, crab, turtle, a kaiju with plates down its back, a little robot, a homunculus, a
   mushroom, a pudgy dragon, a bee that buzzes about, a chameleon that slowly shifts colour, a whale
   shark, a prawn, a meerkat and a friendly skeleton that rattles as it walks). Up close there are little touches: toes
   on the paws, the bear's claws, the deer's fawn spots, the frog's and gecko's spots, suckers down the
   octopus's tentacles. Each animal belongs to one player: animals someone
   else has, or is looking at right now, are greyed out with their name, and if two people click the
   same one at the same moment the first gets it and the other is asked to pick again. Animals are
   listed alphabetically, and the one you're looking at stands on a turntable with your pet beside it:
   turn it round with the buttons either side (hold one to keep turning) or drag it.
   Then they turn on their microphone (or just listen). Later, **Customise** on the bar changes your
   name, animal, colour, hat and pet. While a game is on (from the characters being handed out until
   the Storyteller calls it) your animal and its colour stay as they are, so everyone keeps track of
   who's who; the hat and pet can change any time, and once the game is over (or the Storyteller sets
   up a new one) you can be any animal that's free.
3. **Wander around town.** Talk to whoever is near you (full volume within a couple of metres, gone by
   about ten); **hold H** (or the Shout button) to shout, which carries nearly three times as far. A muted
   player's name plate turns red with a crossed-out mic, for everyone. Hold the right mouse button and drag
   to swing the camera round. Step into the tavern, the bakery, the church, the clock tower or anyone's
   house to talk privately: going in takes a moment at the door, and a ding-dong tells everyone inside
   someone's coming. The map shows everyone in each building. The clock tower has a door on every side (you
   come back out of the one you went in by), into the clock room: the dials glow from behind (showing the real time, backwards), the movement ticks once a
   second with its pendulum swinging, and the bell rope jumps whenever the bell rings.
   - **The Velvet Horn**, a night club, has Big Moo the minotaur on the door: talk to him (E) and he lets
     you in. Inside, the dance floor lights up to the beat and anyone at the jukebox picks one of six
     songs, which everyone inside hears in time (and faintly outside the door).
   - **The Lucky Raven** casino: a pet shop, blackjack against the dealer, Texas hold'em between players
     and roulette, all for real money from your profile and run by the Storyteller's browser so nobody can
     cheat. Sitting at a table takes you to a seat's-eye view (drag with the right button to look round).
   - **The Pet Pit**, down a trapdoor in the tavern: challenge someone and your pets fight in the ring.
     Each round both pick in secret: **Attack** (1 damage, plus any charge), **Block** (stops an attack
     and hits back for 2, but not two rounds running) or **Charge** (+1 on your next attack, up to 3).
     Five hearts each; the winner gets $2 (up to five prizes a day), and the wins go up on the chalk board.
   - **The idea box** beside the noticeboard takes ideas and requests for the Storyteller, who gets
     them in an **Ideas** list on their top bar.
   - **The confession booth** stands in the church's front corner (and two more stand among the trees): two closets side by side with a
     latticed grille between them. Step into either side (E at its curtain) to sit inside. Whoever is in
     one side hears only whoever is in the other, and nobody outside hears them, even standing right by
     the booth. One person to a side; walking off (or E again) steps you back out. While someone sits on
     each side, the booth's velvet curtains draw across both doorways, and they open again as soon as
     either steps out.
   - **The post office**, beside the road just south of the meeting circle, has a letterbox for every
     player with their name on it. Write a letter there by day (signed, or anonymous) and post it in
     someone's letterbox; a little red flag goes up on it. It arrives that night, and they open it in bed
     (a **Letters** button appears when they have some). Each player is only ever sent their own letters,
     and an anonymous one never carries who wrote it. A few letters a day each.
   - **Messenger pigeons**: the **Pigeon** button on a player's menu sends a pigeon to whoever they choose.
     It flies in to wherever that player is (even indoors), lands on their shoulder and tells them who wants
     to speak to them and where that person is ("in the church", "by the pond"), then bursts into confetti.
     Anyone nearby sees it land, but only the one it came for learns who sent it. By day only, and a pigeon
     needs twenty seconds' rest between flights.
4. **Daytime jobs**, each a little minigame:
   - **Bartender** at the Raven's Rest: pour drinks onto the bar for anyone to pick up.
   - **Laundry** at the washhouse: scrub the mud out and hang it on the line.
   - **Fishing** off the pier at the pond: pick a lure, cast, strike on the tug (one, two or three
     marks, the big ones bite fast), then reel while keeping the line out of the red. Fish come in
     common, uncommon, rare and legendary sizes; a rare or legendary catch gets a fanfare.
   - **Baking** at the bakery: knead, then pull it out of the oven when it's golden.
   - **Gardening** in the beds around the square: plant and water a flower, and watch it grow.
   - **Darts** in the tavern, **breaking bread** into even halves and thirds and **pouring wine** into
     a moving chalice at the church lectern, **chopping firewood** at the woodpile, **stone skimming**
     at the pond bank and **tossing a coin** into the well.
   - **Keeping time** at the clock in the tower: tap along with the pendulum's tick-tock (a new tempo
     every go, 70 to 100 beats a minute), then the case shuts, the ticking stops and you keep tapping in
     time for eight more ticks on your own. Every tap moves the clock on, and says if it was early, late
     or spot on. The score is how close you kept to the real beat; a steady lag of up to 30ms, measured
     while you tap along, is let off, since screens, speakers and keyboards all run a little behind.
   You only need to be near a game's spot to start it: the prompt shows up from a few steps away. At
   the washhouse the roof fades when you walk underneath, but the washing stays solid.
   Three stars take real skill: drinks and watering run on a moment after you let go, the timing games
   speed up as you go, and the second-best results only get two. Every game has a score. Beat the town's best and it goes on the noticeboard as a record, with a
   toast for everyone; your own personal bests are kept on your machine. Hold something and you can
   sip it, give it to someone (G) or put it away (Q).
5. **The Storyteller carries the grimoire.** Press **Open grimoire** (or Tab) and the camera swoops in
   over your shoulder as the book opens in your hands; its pages hold the circle of seats, the bag, the
   night order and everything else from the Grimoire. Fill the bag, deal characters and press **Show
   players their characters**. Close it to float off again. Put the Drunk in the bag as it is: as at
   a real table it goes out as a Townsfolk that isn't in play (change which when handing out), the
   player only ever sees that Townsfolk, and your grimoire shows their Townsfolk token with an "Is the
   Drunk" reminder beside it (the Marionette works the same way).
   - **Killed in the night**: when you kill a player at night, a hooded figure with glowing eyes comes in
     at their bedroom door, glides to the bedside and does it one of ten ways (a dagger, a pillow, a
     poison vial, a scythe, a giant mallet, a jack-in-the-box, a lightning bolt, a flock of ravens, a
     candle snuffer or a trapdoor; all ten come round before any repeats), then vanishes in a puff of smoke. Only that player sees it (and you, if
     you're at their bedside); everyone else finds out at dawn as before.
   - **Night**: everyone is tucked up in bed in their own bedroom, fire out, moon and stars in the
     window, and nobody can get up until morning. Press E for a go at **counting sheep**: how many white
     sheep jumped the fence, from either side? Every jump counts as one; the odd goat or wolf slips
     through too, and they don't count. Get it right and you wear a **gold star** over your head all the next day. One go a night.
     On the bedroom wall hangs a chart of **who sits where** round the circle, laid out as the
     Storyteller's grimoire has it (first seat at the top, going clockwise; names only), and a **Who sits
     where** button in bed opens it up close, so "clockwise" means the same to everyone before the first
     meeting. A night card with the Clockwise or Anticlockwise sign (the Shugenja's) shows the same circle.
     The Storyteller wakes players with the same tools as the Grimoire, and can float to a bedside to talk
     to its owner privately. The **night guide** at the top of the Storyteller's screen walks through
     the night order for just the characters in play: **Wake** opens the wake screen for whoever has
     the character, with their question ready ("Who do you want to poison?"), and **Next** (there or
     in the wake screen) puts them back to sleep, ticks them off and wakes the next, all the way to
     **Bring the dawn**. **Skip** passes over someone (a dead Monk, say), the back arrow undoes a
     tick, and the arrow at the top tucks the guide away to a small tab. It shares its ticks with
     the night order in the side panel.
   - **Day**: everyone comes back outside. Deaths in the night are announced at dawn, and dead players
     become ghosts of their animal: they can still walk around and talk.
   - **Ring the bell** and a bell call pops up on every player's screen, wherever they are. Nobody is
     pulled out of what they're doing: **Take my seat** goes straight to their own stool around the
     clock tower, in the same order as the grimoire, or **I'll walk over** lets them finish up and sit
     down on reaching their stool (ringed in gold until they do). Seated, their camera pulls back behind
     their stool to look down into the middle of the circle, so everyone can see everyone (scroll to
     pull it in or out a little). The count under the bell is of players sat on their own stool, and
     **Seat everyone** there (or **Sit them on their stool** in a seat menu) brings anyone still missing
     straight to their stool. Someone who reloads mid-meeting goes straight back to their stool.
     Once everyone is there the bell stops but the meeting carries on, everyone staying put on their
     own stool (they can still wave, cheer and so on from their seat), until you
     press **End meeting** (in the scene bar or under the bell) or night falls. If you reorder the
     seats, players move along to their new stools. During a meeting everyone at the square hears
     everyone else. Nominations and votes play out in 3D: a sign over the tower says who is nominated
     and the count, the red and blue hands on the cobbles sweep from stool to stool, and a big paw goes
     up over anyone voting. Executing the player on the block (mark them, then Kill) plays out at the
     gallows for the whole town. First everyone gets five seconds to vote on how: the gallows, a firing
     squad, the guillotine, the electric chair, a missile, a spit roast, a kaiju, a plane crash, a giant
     pigeon or the moon. A wheel spins, each slice its share of the votes, and lands on what happens
     (you can also just pick one). The hanging is watched from in front; whatever it is, the ghost floats
     up out of it afterwards. For someone who survives being executed (the Devil's
     Advocate's choice, the Fool, a Tea Lady's neighbour), **Execute, but they survive** in their seat
     menu plays the same, but with a lucky escape (the rope snaps, the blade jams, the squad misses...).
   - **Nominating**: by day, every living player has a **Nominate** button, in their menu and by their
     stool at a meeting. They choose who (anyone, dead or alive, themselves included), once a day, and
     nobody can be nominated twice in a day. You get a box: **Start the nomination** sets it up on the
     clock and opens the grimoire to run the vote, or **Decline**. The player sees it waiting, can take
     it back, and is told when you start it or don't (and why, when someone else's nomination of the
     same player got there first).
   - **The end of the game**: when every Demon is dead (good) or only two players are left with the
     Demon among them (evil), you're asked whether the game is over; "Not yet" holds the question
     until something changes (a Scarlet Woman, a starpass). You can also call it either way in
     Settings. Everyone gets a fanfare, confetti and fireworks over the square, every character is
     revealed, and the whole town sits in the ring on their stools: the winners dance, the losers
     have a little cry. A **Game recap** button then shows who was who (and what the Drunk thought
     they were), how each player died, every day's nominations, votes, executions and Slayer
     claims, a few honours (first to fall, most suspected, quickest hand, biggest catch) and, for
     each player, how many of their guesses were right. **Carry on playing** takes it back.
   - **Who's who**: the top of each player's **Notepad** lists every other player with two token
     slots: the character they claim, and the one you think they really are. Click a slot to choose
     the character; a guess that differs from the claim gets a red ring. Only that player sees it,
     and it's kept in their own browser.
   - **The Slayer**: when the script has one, every living player gets a red **Slayer** button on their
     menu during the day. Anyone can use it once a game to claim to be the Slayer and choose a player
     (dead ones too, for a Zombuul that has already "died");
     the whole town sees the claim. You get a box for every claim, real or not, and nothing happens
     until you answer, so the wait gives nobody away. For someone who isn't the Slayer the box only
     offers "Nothing happens". For the real Slayer it shows who they chose and their character, and
     you choose **Kill** or **Nothing happens** (say no if the Slayer is drunk or poisoned). Either
     way the real Slayer gets the No Ability reminder.
   - **The Spy** sees a snapshot of the grimoire when woken from the night order; add one to any wake
     with the book button in the wake screen.
   - **Characters that change**: the night guide fills in each step for the characters in play,
     including the Magician and the Lunatic in the evil team's first-night information, the Shugenja's
     direction, and the Barber, Hatter, Imp and Fang Gu changing characters. After changing someone's
     character, **Tell them they're the X** opens the wake screen ready to show them (a Barber swap
     keeps each player's alignment). When a Demon dies at night that could hand on (the Imp, the Fang
     Gu), the end-of-game question offers who takes over instead. The real Goblin's seat menu has
     **Claimed to be the Goblin**, and a claimed Goblin who's executed that day suggests evil wins. The
     Godfather's and Fang Gu's Outsider counts are allowed for in the bag. The Fang Gu stays out of
     games with 6 or fewer players: it can't go in the bag, random bags never pick it, and the bag and
     the script picker say why. If seats go after it's in the bag, the bag says so and offers to swap
     it for another Demon (trading its extra Outsider back for a Townsfolk).
   - **Waking someone at night** drifts you to their bedside; the player sees a dream bubble beside
     their bed and you're told when they've read it.
   - **Around town**: signs float over the things to do, the well gives water, the noticeboard has the
     day's news, the churchyard gains a stone for each of the dead, and the little speaker by a name
     shows who can hear you. The big map in the corner shows where everyone is.
   - **Hold B** (or the megaphone button) to speak to the whole town at once.
   - **The scene bar** (bottom left) puts everyone to bed, brings the dawn or calls a town meeting in
     one press, sets the weather (clear, mist or rain) and starts a **day timer**. Everyone sees the
     timer as a sun crossing a little arc at the top of the screen, and the bell can ring itself when
     the sun sets.
   - **Click anyone in the town** to open their seat menu right there (wake, mark, kill, float over),
     including **Bring them to me**, which brings that player to stand beside you.
6. **Hats.** Gold stars, town records, nights in bed and a walk as a ghost unlock hats (a beanie for
   everyone, then a flower crown, party hat, witch hat, wizard hat, top hat, halo and crown). Every
   job has its own hat too, for doing it perfectly (all five logs split, three stars, a wish come
   true): a lumberjack beanie for chopping wood, a sou'wester for fishing, a mitre for the bread or
   wine at church, a chef's hat for baking, a bowler for the bar, a straw sun hat for gardening, a
   headscarf for the laundry, a feathered cap for darts, a sailor's cap for skimming stones, a
   leprechaun hat for the wishing well and a clockmaker's cap (with a little clock, a cog and a loupe)
   for keeping time. A new hat pops up on screen with your animal wearing it and a
   **Wear it now** button. Hats are remembered in each player's own browser and chosen in the animal
   picker (or with **Customise**); locked ones say how to earn them. Every hat you own makes you a
   touch quicker about town, walking and running: 0.1% each (40 hats is 4%).
7. **Achievements.** Over 70 more things to do, with more than thirty more hats: win on the good team (a laurel wreath)
   or the evil team (devil horns), catch 10 fish (a pirate's tricorn), run a whole lap of the ring
   road (a jogger's headband), talk with someone through the confession booth (a confessor's candle),
   post 5 letters (a postie's cap), plus surviving to the end, being hanged, being nominated,
   nominating, voting, using your ghost vote, a Slayer claim, talking for 10 minutes, visiting all
   four public buildings in a day, gifts, snacks, wishes, fan mail and games played. Then: guess three
   characters right in your notepad by the end of a game (a deerstalker), catch eight kinds of fish (a
   trainer's cap) and get three stars at six different jobs (a mortarboard), and plenty of secrets: a
   fez, a tin foil hat, a Mad Hatter's hat, a cowboy hat, a robot helmet, a traffic cone and a
   jack-o'-lantern (dance among the graves as a ghost), each for something a bit silly; and twelve
   more pop-culture ones, from a viking helmet (shout three times in ten seconds) to an adventurer's
   fedora (reel in a silver eel). Secret ones stay a question mark until found. The
   **Awards** button lists them all. Anything new pops up straight away, but waits for a hanging, a
   night visit, a vote or a banner on screen to finish; one earned by dying waits eight seconds more,
   so you see it all.
8. **Profiles and money.** Your hats, achievements, stars and money are kept by the Storyteller under
   your name, so they follow you to any device (and a copy stays in your browser). You earn money for
   finishing a game ($10), being on the winning team ($25), surviving ($5), achievements ($5, or $10
   for a secret one) and three-star jobs ($2, up to $20 a day), and everyone starts with $25; spend it at
   the casino. **Pets are bought at the casino's pet shop** ($20 to $80; 47 of them, many fond takes on pop
   culture favourites). The Storyteller's **Players' profiles** (in Settings) shows everyone's money and
   achievements, can give or take either, and exports and imports the lot.
9. **Nicknames.** Tap anyone in your Notepad's Who's who to give them a nickname that only you see, on
   their name plate and everywhere else. Nobody else's screen (nor the grimoire) changes.

### Controls

| Key | Does |
|---|---|
| W A S D, arrows, or click the ground | Walk (Shift to run) |
| E | Use things: jobs, benches, beds, the bar |
| 1 to 6 | Wave, dance, cheer, laugh, cry, sit |
| 7, then 1 to 7 | Dances: twerk, griddy, floss, robot, moonwalk, horse ride, macarena |
| H (hold) | Shout |
| Right mouse button, drag | Swing the camera round |
| G / Q | Give what you're holding / put it away |
| M / V | Mute / hold to talk (if push-to-talk is on) |
| Scroll | Zoom in and out |
| Tab | Storyteller: open or close the grimoire |
| B | Storyteller: speak to everyone |

### Sound

Everything you hear is made in the browser: the bell, the drumroll, a night tune in the bedroom, and
a soft ambience around town (a gentle tune and birds by day, crickets and an owl by night, water and
frogs by the pond, the rope creaking at the gallows, a fire in the tavern and the bakery, a hush in
the church, the clock ticking in the tower). It sits well under the voices and can be turned off in the player settings.

The cog by the mic button has push-to-talk (hold V), everyone's volume, and a slider for **each
person**, for the one who's too loud or too quiet. It's kept in that browser.

### The look of the town

Everything here is drawn on each player's own screen and sends nothing over the network. Soft shadows
gather where walls, trees, benches and furniture meet the ground or the floor; clouds drift over with
their shadows sliding across the town; puddles spread in the rain (with rings from the drops) and dry
up afterwards; running along the dirt paths kicks up little puffs of dust; sunbeams slant in through
the church windows on a fine day; and flames, lamps and the clock tower's dials have a soft glow,
stronger after dark. The glows and clouds are left out on low graphics.

### When something goes wrong

If anything in a player's game breaks, they get a note with **Reload** (they keep their seat) rather
than a blank screen, and the Storyteller is told quietly, with the details under **Problems in
players' games** in Settings. If the browser resets the graphics card, the town says so and comes
back on low graphics.

### Slow laptops

The town turns shadows off and drops the resolution by itself if a machine can't keep up (a notice
says so). Add `?fx=low` to the address to start that way.

## How the connection works

Browsers find the Storyteller through the free [PeerJS](https://peerjs.com/) lobby server, then talk
directly. The Storyteller's browser tells each player where everyone is ten times a second, but only
who moved since the last message (people somewhere else, who only show on the map, a couple of times a
second at most), so standing still costs nothing and a night in bed costs almost nothing. Voice uses
the same lobby: each browser opens a voice line only to people in or near earshot (and to the
Storyteller), keeps it open but silent for a couple of minutes after they wander off (so the next
meeting is instant rather than everyone dialling everyone at once), dials new people a couple at a
time, nearest first, and sets each voice's volume from how far apart the two animals are, so a big
group doesn't swamp anyone's connection. Voices are sent as mono speech at a modest bitrate, a little
lower again in a crowd, and a quiet microphone sends almost nothing. A player whose connection can't
keep up skips a beat of everyone's positions rather than falling further behind. If the lobby is
down, the game falls back to a pool of public Nostr relays (via
[Trystero](https://github.com/dmotz/trystero)), but voice needs the lobby. Each player only ever
receives their own character and information.

Each player's round trip to the Storyteller is measured every couple of seconds. Anyone slower than
0.8 seconds shows as **slow** on the Storyteller's room chip (hover for who and how slow) and with an
amber dot by their name in the grimoire, and sees **Slow connection** themselves, with tips. A busy
computer shows up the same way as a busy line. The Storyteller's **Game** settings list every player's
connection live: the network's own round trip (green under 150 ms) and whether they're **direct**,
through a **relay** (and which), or on the **backup route** (no voice).

If the lobby doesn't work for you, run your own PeerServer and put its address in `docs/config.js`.

**A relay for strict networks.** Some networks (workplaces, universities, phone hotspots) block direct
connections, and players on them can't reach anyone without a TURN relay. The game falls back to the
free shared PeerJS relay, which is often busy. For a reliable one, make a free account with a TURN
provider (for example [Metered](https://www.metered.ca/stun-turn) or Cloudflare Calls), and paste the
username and credential into `relay` in `docs/config.js` (and `public/config.js`, so the next build keeps
it). The file shows the format.

## Changing the site

```sh
npm install
npm run dev        # http://localhost:5173
npm run build      # rebuilds docs/
```

Commit the rebuilt `docs/` folder and GitHub Pages updates within a minute or two.
Each build keeps the previous 14 builds' files in `docs/assets` (listed in `docs/assets/deploys.json`), so
anyone still playing on a page from before the update can open dialogs and minigames as usual.

To test with several browsers on one computer, run `npm run peer` (a local lobby on port 9000) and
add `?peer=127.0.0.1:9000` to every address. `node tools/test-town.mjs` runs a full game with a
Storyteller and four players in headless Chrome and saves screenshots. Only one player's page draws as it
goes; the others are opened with `?debug&nodraw`, which does everything (voice included) except draw, so the
whole game runs in about a quarter of an hour on a machine without a graphics card.
